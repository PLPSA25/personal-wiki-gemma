# Supply and Demand

Supply and demand describe the relationship between the availability of a good and the desire for it in a market. Equilibrium occurs where the amount supplied equals the amount demanded, and changes in price or other factors cause shifts in these relationships. Elasticity measures how sensitive quantity demanded or supplied is to changes in price.

## Key points

- Above the equilibrium price there is excess supply that pushes price down; below it excess demand pushes price up. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 4]])
- Demand is marginal benefit; own-price changes are movements and everything else is a shift. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 4]])
- Shifters of demand include prices of substitutes and complements, income (normal goods rise with income, luxury goods rise more than proportionally, inferior goods fall), features, ecosystem and expectations. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 4]])
- Elasticity of demand is the percent change in quantity divided by the percent change in price, a unit-free measure. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 4]])
- If its absolute value is above 1 demand is elastic (quantity responds a lot); below 1 it is inelastic. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 4]])
- A real example I have seen is the labor market for people with AI and technology skills. As companies started investing more seriously in generative AI, they also began looking for more people who could help build, implement, and manage these technologies. This increased the demand for AI-skilled workers, shifting the labor demand curve to the right. ([[raw/Supply and Demand Shocks.docx|Supply and Demand Shocks]])
