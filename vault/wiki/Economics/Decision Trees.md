# Decision Trees

Decision trees are a way to model decisions by mapping out choices, chance events, and potential outcomes. By drawing forward and solving backward, one can determine the expected value of different paths. The value of information is determined by how much knowledge changes the expected outcome of a decision.

## Key points

- A tree has decision nodes (squares, where you choose), chance nodes (circles, where the world chooses with probabilities) and payoffs at the end of every path. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 9]])
- Draw forward and solve backward: take the expected value at each circle and the best branch at each square. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 9]])
- Information is worth something only if it is pivotal. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 9]])
- Valuable information is pivotal, it is worth most when you are close to indifferent and when you know least. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 9]])
- Option value exists when waiting is possible, valuable (pivotal) information will arrive, and the action is at least partly irreversible (a one-way door). ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 9]])
