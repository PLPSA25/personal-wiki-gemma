# Long-Run Competition

In the long run, free entry and competition cause economic profit to be driven to zero in both perfect and monopolistic competition. Sustained profitability requires a durable advantage that rivals cannot easily copy or mimic. This advantage often stems from factors like natural endowments, scale, or unique business models.

## Key points

- In the long run, economic profit goes to zero in both perfect and monopolistic competition. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 8]])
- The escape from zero economic profit is a durable, hard-to-mimic advantage or genuinely restricted entry. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 8]])
- Durable advantages include natural endowments (geology, location), economies of scale or scope at a size rivals cannot reach, compounding learning or process advantages, favorable rules or subsidies, and a genuinely different business model. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 8]])
- Price-making alone is not enough for profitability in the long run. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 8]])
- If price is above minimum average cost, firms make money and others enter, pushing price down; if below, firms lose money and some exit, pushing price up. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 8]])
