# Cost Disease

Cost disease occurs when productivity in a sector does not increase at the same rate as wages elsewhere, causing costs to rise. This phenomenon is illustrated by the increased opportunity cost of labor in certain sectors. The concept relates to how changes in productivity and wages affect the cost structure of an economy.

## Key points

- When productivity in a sector does not keep pace with rising wages elsewhere, its costs climb. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 2]])
- Playing a Beethoven quartet takes the same 2.66 hours as in 1826, but the opportunity cost of that labor is about 23 times higher (real wages from $1.14 to $26.44). ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 2]])
- Higher education, health care, the performing arts and personal services are affected; fast technology growth and the ability to shift inputs protect against it. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 2]])
- Cost advantages are usually hard to maintain (think Moneyball). ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 8]])
- Paying higher wages does not automatically mean higher cost: Costco's wage premium buys lower turnover and more sales per employee. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 3]])
