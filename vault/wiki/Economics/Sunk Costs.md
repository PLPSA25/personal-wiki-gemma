# Sunk Costs

Sunk costs are costs that have already been incurred and cannot be changed, and they should be irrelevant when making future decisions. Ignoring these past expenditures can lead to poor forward-looking choices. It is important to focus on future costs and benefits rather than past spending.

## Key points

- Sunk costs are costs that cannot be changed are irrelevant to forward-looking decisions. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 2]])
- If you lose a $50 ticket at the door, attending costs $50, not $100. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 2]])
- Whether to leave a disappointing event does not depend on whether you paid $50 or $500. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 2]])
- Sunk costs concern a door that has already closed; irreversibility also matters looking forward. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 2]])
- Past investments should not affect future decisions. ([[raw/Sunk Cost Reasoning.docx|Sunk Cost Reasoning]])
- An expense is a direct cost of a part only if making the part causes it; treat unallocable overhead as sunk for the operating decision (it bears on exit, not on the next unit). ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 3]])
