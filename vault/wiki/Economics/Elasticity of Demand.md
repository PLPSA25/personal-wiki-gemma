# Elasticity of Demand

Elasticity of demand measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price. If the absolute value of the elasticity is above 1, demand is considered elastic, meaning quantity responds significantly to price changes. This concept determines whether a price shock affects total revenue more through quantity or price.

## Key points

- Elasticity of demand is the percent change in quantity divided by the percent change in price, a unit-free measure. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 4]])
- If its absolute value is above 1 demand is elastic (quantity responds a lot); below 1 it is inelastic. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 4]])
- The more elastic the market, the more of a shock lands on quantity rather than price. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 4]])
- Elasticity grows with time, so an oil spike such as the 2026 Strait of Hormuz disruption lands mostly on price in the short run and gives most of it back in the long run. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 4]])
- Elasticity sets whether a shock lands on price or quantity. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 4]])
- With MR = P(1 + 1/e) (e being the elasticity of demand) the optimal price is P = MC / (1 + 1/e): more elastic demand means a lower markup. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 5]])
