# Monopoly Pricing

Monopoly pricing involves setting a price where marginal revenue equals marginal cost. A monopolist uses this relationship, along with the demand curve, to determine the optimal output and price. The concept also involves how different pricing structures, like two-part tariffs, affect value capture.

## Key points

- With a two-part tariff (entry fee F plus per-unit price P), the optimal choice is P = MC and F = the consumer surplus at that price. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 5]])
- The optimal price is P = MC / (1 + 1/e): more elastic demand means a lower markup. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 5]])
- A price taker does not affect price, so MR = P and the rule collapses to P = MC. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 5]])
- The rule is always MR = MC, then read the price off the demand curve. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 5]])
- Monopoly power is the ability to move the market price; every firm sets MR = MC; price depends on marginal cost and demand, not fixed cost. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 5]])
- A monopolist never prices in the inelastic region of demand, so it does not score 1 [in the Lerner index]. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 5]])
