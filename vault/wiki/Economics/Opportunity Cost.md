# Opportunity Cost

Opportunity cost is the value of the most highly valued alternative that is given up when making a choice. It is a crucial concept for forward-looking economic decisions because it accounts for the value of alternatives rather than just the expenditure. Economic cost is calculated by subtracting sunk costs and including opportunity costs.

## Key points

- The opportunity cost of an action is the value of the most highly valued alternative given up. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 2]])
- Opportunity cost of time is a relevant economic cost. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 2]])
- Nothing you own is free: the Tokyo firm's basement farm costs its expenditure plus the profit forgone from the next best use of that real estate. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 2]])
- Economic cost = expenditure (accounting cost) - sunk costs + opportunity costs. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 2]])
- Accurately measuring internal capacity was difficult because employees who appeared available could already be supporting other projects or lacked the required skills, and future maintenance needs were uncertain. ([[raw/Relevant Cost.docx|Relevant Cost]])
