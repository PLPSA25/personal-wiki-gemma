# Standard Error

The standard error measures the dispersion of a statistic from sample to sample, indicating the uncertainty in an answer. It is calculated by dividing the population standard deviation by the square root of the sample size. The standard error is a measure of the unavoidable uncertainty involved when using a sample instead of the entire population.

## Key points

- For the sample mean it equals sigma divided by the square root of n (the "square root rule"). ([[raw/Data and Decisions Lectures|Data and Decisions Lectures, Lecture 2]])
- The standard error is proportional to the population standard deviation, inversely proportional to the square root of the sample size, and does not depend on the population size. ([[raw/Data and Decisions Lectures|Data and Decisions Lectures, Lecture 4]])
- When only the sample standard deviation s is known (not sigma), the standard error is estimated as s divided by the square root of n. ([[raw/Data and Decisions Lectures|Data and Decisions Lectures, Lecture 5]])
- A confidence interval needs the sample mean and the standard error. ([[raw/Data and Decisions Lectures|Data and Decisions Lectures, Lecture 5]])
- The margin of error (ME) is an informal 95% interval that replaces the t value by 2: ME = 2 x standard error. ([[raw/Data and Decisions Lectures|Data and Decisions Lectures, Lecture 5]])
