## Economics

- [[Cost Disease]] - Cost disease occurs when productivity in a sector does not increase at the same rate as wages elsewhere, causing costs to rise.
- [[Decision Trees]] - Decision trees are a way to model decisions by mapping out choices, chance events, and potential outcomes.
- [[Durable Advantage]] - A durable advantage is an edge that rivals cannot easily copy, which allows a business to maintain profitability in a market where free entry would otherwise drive profits to zero.
- [[Elasticity of Demand]] - Elasticity of demand measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price.
- [[Group Pricing]] - Group pricing is a form of price discrimination where different groups face different prices for the same product.
- [[Long-Run Competition]] - In the long run, free entry and competition cause economic profit to be driven to zero in both perfect and monopolistic competition.
- [[Menu Pricing]] - Menu pricing involves offering buyers a single menu of options, allowing them to self-select based on their preferences.
- [[Monopoly Pricing]] - Monopoly pricing involves setting a price where marginal revenue equals marginal cost.
- [[Opportunity Cost]] - Opportunity cost is the value of the most highly valued alternative that is given up when making a choice.
- [[Option Value]] - Option value arises when waiting is possible and the information that arrives is valuable.
- [[Relevant Cost]] - Relevant cost focuses only on the additional costs that will be incurred as a result of a specific decision.
- [[Sunk Costs]] - Sunk costs are costs that have already been incurred and cannot be changed, and they should be irrelevant when making future decisions.
- [[Supply and Demand]] - Supply and demand describe the relationship between the availability of a good and the desire for it in a market.

- [[Value of Information]] - The value of information is determined by how it changes the expected value of a decision.

## Data Analysis

- [[A-B Testing]] - A-B testing involves randomizing treatment and control status to determine causation.
- [[Causal Inference]] - Causal inference is the process of determining if an action causes an outcome, which is distinct from mere prediction based on association.
- [[Confidence Intervals]] - A confidence interval provides a range of plausible values for a population parameter based on a sample.
- [[Control Limits]] - Control limits are thresholds that form a decision rule, typically a symmetric interval around the mean based on the mean plus or minus a multiple of the standard error.
- [[Hypothesis Testing]] - Hypothesis testing involves setting up a null hypothesis and an alternative hypothesis to determine if there is enough evidence to reject the null.
- [[Linear Regression]] - Linear regression uses ordinary least squares to find the line that minimizes the sum of squared residuals between the data and the fitted line.
- [[Omitted Variable Bias]] - Omitted variable bias occurs when a variable is left out of a model but is correlated with both a variable being controlled for and the outcome.
- [[Sampling Bias]] - Sampling bias occurs when the method used to select a sample systematically favors certain outcomes over others, leading to a non-representative sample of the target population.
- [[Sampling Methods]] - Sampling methods involve selecting items from a larger list called the sampling frame to create a sample.
- [[Standard Error]] - The standard error measures the dispersion of a statistic from sample to sample, indicating the uncertainty in an answer.
