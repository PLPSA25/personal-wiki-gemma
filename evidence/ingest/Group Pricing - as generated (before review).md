# Group Pricing

Group pricing is a form of price discrimination where different groups face different prices for the same product. This requires identifying the groups, charging different prices, and preventing resale among the groups. The profitability depends on genuine differences in willingness to pay across the segments.

## Key points

- Third-degree price discrimination is segment (group) pricing: each group faces one price but prices differ across groups. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 6]])
- When I canceled my Italian Netflix account, Netflix later offered me a lower price to return to the same plan. This is third-degree price discrimination because Netflix divides customers into identifiable groups: former subscribers and current subscribers. ([[raw/Group Pricing.docx|Group Pricing]])
- The seller offers every buyer the same menu of options and buyers self-select. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 7]])
- The three varieties of second-degree price discrimination are versioning (options vary by quality), bundling (options vary by composition), and quantity discounts (options vary by quantity). ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 7]])
- The conditions to price differently across groups are identifying types, separating them by charging different prices, and preventing arbitrage. ([[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 6]])
- Arbitrage is when buyers resell (someone using a senior discount for someone else). ([[raw/Group Pricing.docx|Group Pricing]])
