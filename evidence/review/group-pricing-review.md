# Review finding: a wrong source link in a generated note

Note: `vault/wiki/Economics/Group Pricing.md` (generated by `gemma4:e2b`, status `draft`). Found by me while reviewing the note in Obsidian.
The version exactly as generated is kept in [`../ingest/Group Pricing - as generated (before review).md`](../ingest/Group%20Pricing%20-%20as%20generated%20%28before%20review%29.md).

## What was wrong
1. **Mis-attributed citation.** The key point *"Arbitrage is when buyers resell (someone using a senior discount for someone else)"* linked to my own
   document `Group Pricing.docx`. That document (a Netflix example) never mentions a senior discount. The sentence comes from the lecture summary,
   `Economics Lectures.md`, Lecture 6, lines 263-266 (passage `Economics Lectures.md#27`), which was not even among the passages listed for the note.
2. **Off-topic points.** Two key points described second-degree price discrimination (menu pricing, Lecture 7); that subject belongs to the `Menu Pricing` note.

## Why the harness did not catch it
The validation checks that a cited passage label exists, that every number in a key point appears in the cited passage, that the note uses each required
source, and that links point to real notes. It does **not** check that a passage *supports* a claim in words; the sentence contains no number, so nothing
could fail. This is the "a citation is not proof" limit the assignment describes, and a reason why review by a person is part of the workflow.

## Correction made in the wiki (the original in `raw/` was not touched)
- The arbitrage key point now links to `[[raw/Economics Lectures|Economics Lectures, Lecture 6]]`.
- The two Lecture 7 key points were removed, and the `## Sources` list and the `source_passages` property were updated to match (Lecture 6, lines 263-266 replaces the Lecture 7 entry).
- The edit was applied by Claude at my request after I found the error; I then re-read the result.

## Suggested improvement
Add a check that a key point shares its distinctive words with the passage it cites (or a second yes/no model call per key point, like the ask-mode verifier).
