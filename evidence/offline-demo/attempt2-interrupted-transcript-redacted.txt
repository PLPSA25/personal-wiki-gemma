# Sunk Costs

Sunk cost reasoning occurs when past investments influence future decisions, even when those investments are no longer relevant. This behavior is reinforced by institutional factors like organizational inertia and reputation, rather than being purely a psychological bias. Addressing this involves changing the decision-making process to evaluate options based only on future costs and benefits.

## Key points

- Past investments should not affect future decisions. ([[raw/Sunk Cost Reasoning.docx|Sunk Cost Reasoning]])
- Organizations often continue investing in legacy IT systems because of the resources and organizational effort already committed to them. ([[raw/Sunk Cost Reasoning.docx|Sunk Cost Reasoning]])
- Managers often felt that their reputations were tied to earlier decisions. ([[raw/Sunk Cost Reasoning.docx|Sunk Cost Reasoning]])
- Major previous investments could be reviewed by an independent third-party team that was not involved in the original decision. ([[raw/Sunk Cost Reasoning.docx|Sunk Cost Reasoning]])
